Which GPS Format Should I Use?

Format Best For Pros Watch Outs
GPX Universal tracks and waypoints Simple XML and broad support Can get large with many points
KML Google Earth and route previews Good for styled viewing and map sharing Heavier XML, styling varies by app
TCX Fitness workouts Useful for laps and sensor-rich workouts App support can vary compared to GPX
FIT Garmin and modern wearables Efficient binary format with more sensor detail Needs decoding before manual editing

Suggested Columns (GPS → CSV / XLSX)

  • time — ISO timestamp
  • lat, lon — WGS84 coordinates
  • ele — elevation in meters
  • speed — meters per second or km/h
  • hr — heart rate
  • cadence — steps or RPM
  • dist — cumulative distance
  • lap or segment — optional grouping

If some fields are missing, that’s okay — the converter will include what exists.

Best Practices

  • Normalize timestamps: keep everything in UTC before merging datasets.
  • Reduce noise: smooth unrealistic coordinate spikes before analysis.
  • Downsample: for map sharing, reduce points to keep files smaller.
  • Validate coordinates: make sure latitude and longitude stay in valid ranges.
  • Protect privacy: trim points near home before sharing public tracks.

FAQs & Troubleshooting

Do my files upload anywhere? No. Everything runs locally in your browser.

My FIT file won’t parse. Some devices export different variants. Re-export from the vendor app and try again.

Data looks shifted or empty. Check time zones and make sure lat/lon columns are numeric.

Very large files feel slow. Close heavy tabs and consider splitting or downsampling routes.
